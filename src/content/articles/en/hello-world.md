With many technical problems, I feel as if I fully understand them the moment I finish solving them. When I revisit them later, however, all that remains is scattered code, a few screenshots, and a vague note saying, “I think there was a trap here.”

This blog is my way of pushing back against that.

I want to preserve the problems that genuinely took time and thought: what happened, why I made a particular choice, which approaches failed, and what I would do differently next time.

---

## What I Will Write About

The topics will probably revolve around a few areas, though I do not intend to draw rigid boundaries around them.

- **AI engineering in practice**: LLMs, RAG, agents, VLMs, and the constraints that appear only after these technologies enter real enterprise environments.
- **Product and system design**: How to turn a working demo into a tool people will use and a team can maintain.
- **Developer tools and ways of working**: Tools, frameworks, and practices that have genuinely improved my workflow, including the attempts that failed.
- **Learning and life**: Reading notes, travel, cooking, and any everyday moment worth pausing to think about.

If there is one thread connecting all of these topics, it is probably this: **how something moves from “seems feasible” to “actually useful.”**

---

## How I Want to Write

I do not want to publish only polished collections of supposed standard answers.

Rather than simply telling you what the “best practice” is, I want to make the context and trade-offs clear. Engineering rarely has a best answer that is independent of its circumstances. Change the data, team, or constraints, and the right decision may change completely.

I will therefore try to make each article do three things:

1. Explain the context in which the problem occurred.
2. Preserve the reasoning behind the decision, not only the final result.
3. Clearly identify what remains uncertain or unverified.

Some articles will be comprehensive. Others may be no more than an idea still taking shape. Both are useful; what matters is that writing them down gives the next round of thinking somewhere to begin.

## About Me

I am Bruce. My current focus is AI applications—connecting LLMs, RAG, and agents to real enterprise workflows.

I spent four years in manufacturing and now continue to work on AI adoption across legal, R&D, and knowledge-work scenarios. I often care more about permissions, data quality, user habits, maintenance costs, and failure handling than model leaderboards.

These may not be the most eye-catching parts of an AI project, but they are often what determine whether it survives.
